The story in one breath

What happened in ten weeks

Ari and Lena build the community's payout engine and launch it on a campaign. In week seven the engine double-pays a bounty pool — and Ari, who had been running it on nothing, goes dark. For two weeks a small group keeps the community running, stops the bleeding, and quietly looks after Ari. The audit that follows finds the rule had let the engine team self-approve its own bounties. The community pauses, re-looks, changes the rule, and settles its books. Then v2 ships — with Ari back in it.

One story. Six people. Twenty-two dimensions — every one of them earned by evidence, none of them by hours.

A composite story — real-shaped, not a real incident. The point is the measurement, not the people.

The rules of the reading

What the 22 do instead

  1. One action, a sparse profile. An action can light several dimensions at once; the intensities need not sum to one.

  2. Credit only on evidence. A dimension is credited when the action and its outcome support it — never because the card sounds right.

  3. null is not zero. Unmeasured is recorded as null, and null never decays into zero. When the system cannot yet measure, the debt is the system's — not the member's.

  4. Outcome pays, activity never does. Hours, messages, attendance: never. The effect they produced: yes.

  5. Every credit carries a guard, a tier, and a timing. Continuous, cycle-gated, or settled late — and a third party can recompute any one of them.

The names come from the 22 Major Arcana — a 350-year-old map of action. The measurement is ours; the vocabulary is borrowed.

The shape

26 records · 22 dimensions · 6 people · 10 weeks. No single score, no leaderboard. Every line recomputable from its evidence.

The point of the exercise

Why this story could not be mapped any other way

A single score says

6,214 points. It cannot say who cared, who exposed, who reframed — or that the care happened five weeks before anyone could prove it. The profile, which is the whole point, is gone.

The 22 records say

26 lines. Each with its own evidence, its own guard, its own timing. The care is null for five weeks, then settles late, back-dated. The milestone pays less, because the harm is verified. The exposure pays, unblockable by the accused. The reframe pays, because a decision changed.

If a future story needs a dimension this map lacks, the gap is logged — and the ontology grows on evidence, never on speculation.
What this proves

The completeness test, in action

The design hypothesis says: 22 dimensions are enough to express any meaningful contribution. This story is an instance of that test, run on real-shaped actions.

  1. Distinguished boundaries. All 22 dimensions are active in one story, and each is explainable against its nearest neighbors: Tower (unplanned collapse) vs Death (chosen ending) vs World (completion of the whole); Empress's presence vs Hierophant's competence; Hermit's finding vs Fool's crossing.

  2. Useful combinations. One action lights several at once: the incident is Tower + Moon + Hierophant + Chariot's harm at the same time — without flattening into "an incident happened."

  3. No gaps found. Every beat of the story is expressed as a combination. If one had not been, the gap would be logged as a boundary revision — never as a failure of the members.

And the fairness guarantees, observed live: null → settled late (the care) · harm reduces the reward (the milestone) · no leaderboard (the story ends in attribution, not ranking).
